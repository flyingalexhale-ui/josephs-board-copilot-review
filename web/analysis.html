Gap scorecard

A gap is when a stock opens at 9:30 am 1% or more away from the day before's 4 pm close (the move you see after-hours, overnight and pre-market).

Held = it still closed on the gap side of the old close that day. Filled = during the day it came all the way back to the old close. Ran = it closed even further in the gap's direction than it opened.

Gap today = before 9:30 am, the move so far vs the 4 pm close; after 9:30, the actual opening gap.

"Gaps like this" counts past gaps in the same direction and of a similar size (1-2%, 2-4%, 4%+) as today's. The past year comes from Yahoo daily prices; your record is kept by the Mac from its own prices each day at 4:05 pm and grows over time.

Relative strength vs SPY and QQQ

Is the stock really moving, or just following the market? SPY is the S&P 500 fund; QQQ is the Nasdaq 100 fund.

vs SPY = the stock's move minus SPY's move. +2.0 means it did 2 percentage points better than the market. Negative = weaker than the market.

Beta = how much the stock usually moves when the market moves 1% (last 90 days). Beta 1.5: when SPY goes up 1%, it usually goes up about 1.5%.

Expected = beta × today's SPY move. Own move = what's left after taking that out: the part of today's move that is about the stock itself (news, earnings...). A big own move is worth a look.

Today = since the last 4 pm close (so it includes after-hours, overnight and pre-market). If SPY or QQQ aren't on a board, their futures (ES, NQ) stand in.

Which stocks move together

Correlation compares the daily moves of two stocks over the last 60 trading days. 1.0 = they always move the same way; 0 = no link; negative = they tend to move opposite ways.

0.7 or more = they move together most days. Holding several of those is close to one bigger bet on the same thing; if one falls, the others probably will too.

Options: max pain and put/call

From Yahoo's free, delayed options data (about 15 minutes late), for the nearest weekly or monthly expiry.

Max pain = the share price at which, on expiry day, option buyers would collect the least (and option sellers keep the most). Some traders think big option sellers nudge the price toward it near expiry. It's a hint, not a promise, and matters most in the last day or two.

Put/call ratio = puts ÷ calls. Puts are bets on a fall (or insurance), calls are bets on a rise. Above 1 = more puts: traders are cautious or hedging. Below 0.7 = more calls: traders are upbeat. Open interest = all contracts still open; volume = traded today.

Call / put walls = the strikes with the most open contracts. A big call wall above the price can act like a ceiling; a big put wall below like a floor.

Free sources only: the board's own prices, Yahoo daily prices and Yahoo delayed options. For information, not advice.
